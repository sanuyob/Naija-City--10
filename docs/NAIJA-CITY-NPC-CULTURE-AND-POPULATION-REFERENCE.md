# Naija City — NPC Culture, Occupations and District Population Reference

**Purpose:** grounded visual and behavioral direction for human NPCs in the fictional Naija City districts. This is a production reference, not a census or a claim that every resident of a real neighbourhood fits one profile.

**Research approach:** contemporary Lagos photojournalism and street-style reporting; district and commercial-area guides; academic research on everyday work and transport; Ekiti State government cultural/economic information; and the African Development Bank's Eko Atlantic environmental/social assessment. Evidence is used to guide plausible character variety. Where a source describes a district's commercial role rather than its full population, the NPC mix below is an explicit design inference.

## 1. Core art direction

- Make people look like contemporary Nigerians in a real city, not a single pan-African costume set.
- Clothing should follow occupation, weather, activity and time of day. Use everyday clothing as the default; reserve traditional attire for family events, ceremonies, worship occasions and selected individual preferences.
- Mix ages, genders, body types, hairstyles, skin tones, income levels and levels of formality within every district. Do not visually equate wealth, occupation, ethnicity or personality.
- A uniform should only appear where a job reasonably uses one: security, facility maintenance, hospitality, some retail, health services, traffic control and construction.
- Do not use one model repeatedly with only a colour swap. Vary silhouette, hair, accessories, footwear, carried objects, animation and gait.
- Keep props small and readable at mobile camera distance: backpack, tote, shopping bag, phone, clipboard, tool bag, handcart, produce tray, reflective vest, cap, umbrella, delivery bag.

## 2. District reference

### Yaba — market, education, technology and creative street culture

**Evidence:** reporting on Yaba Market documents traders selling fruit, phone accessories, fabrics and second-hand clothing, alongside young people who visit for vintage clothing and streetwear. Contemporary Lagos photography also documents designers, snack vendors, shop workers, students and commuters.

**NPC occupations:** market traders, fruit/snack sellers, phone-accessory sellers, fabric/clothing vendors, students, lecturers, tech staff, startup workers, barbers, tailors, mechanics, couriers, danfo passengers and drivers, street-food cooks, cleaners and security staff.

**Wardrobe direction:** relaxed tees, polos, button-downs, denim, cargo trousers, skirts, trainers, slides, backpacks and cross-body bags; expressive streetwear and thrifted/vintage combinations for some younger creatives; practical breathable clothes for traders; aprons or work shirts for food sellers and mechanics; neat casual shirts for shop and tech staff. Printed fabrics and occasion outfits appear as individual choices, not a district-wide uniform.

**Street behavior and props:** browsing stalls, checking phones, comparing prices, carrying stock, arranging clothes, loading cartons, waiting for transport, walking in pairs, chatting outside shops, skateboarding in selected youth spaces, and quick food purchases. Use realistic sidewalk congestion without blocking every path.

**Source basis:** [New York Times — Yaba Market and young women](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2023/02/01/style/lagos-nigeria-women.html); [The New Yorker — Lagos through everyday photography](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/photo-booth/lagos-is-a-vortex-of-energy); [Teen Vogue — Lagos street style](https://www.teenvogue.com/story/lagos-street-style-2023).

### Alimosho — residential streets, local markets and transport interchange

**Evidence:** research on market women in Alimosho documents regular trips to regional markets, movement of goods, transport waiting and the constraints of small-freight movement. Local reporting on Ikotun Market describes a large mix of food, clothing, footwear, electronics and household-goods traders, with suppliers, transport operators and shoppers around the market.

**NPC occupations:** produce and food traders, market porters, shopkeepers, bus/minibus drivers and conductors, dispatch riders, mechanics, electricians, plumbers, tailors, hairdressers, POS operators, food vendors, teachers, nurses, security guards, cleaners, parents, school pupils and commuters.

**Wardrobe direction:** everyday shirts, polos, tees, jeans, skirts, simple dresses, practical sandals and trainers; market traders in varied prints, blouses, wrappers or skirts, headscarves and sun protection; mechanics and tradespeople in work shirts, coveralls or durable trousers; transport staff in practical casual clothing or route/company shirts; selected workers in reflective gear. Avoid making every trader wear the same wrapper or every driver wear the same uniform.

**Street behavior and props:** loading and unloading goods, carrying sacks and cartons, handcarts, shopping bags, waiting at bus stops, calling passengers, repairing vehicles, serving food, handling mobile payments and walking between home and shops.

**Source basis:** [International Journal of Research and Innovation in Social Science — Alimosho market-women freight study](https://rsisinternational.org/journals/ijriss/articles/analysis-of-the-intra-urban-challenges-of-small-freight-distribution-among-market-women-in-alimosho-local-government-area-of-lagos-state-nigeria/); [Freedom Online — Ikotun Market](https://freedomonline.com.ng/lagos-this-is-ikotun-market-with-8400-lock-up-shops-more-than-10000-traders-by-nimah-akindele/).

### Lekki — gated residential estates, mixed retail and service work

**Evidence:** reporting on Lekki includes residential estates, shopping malls, facility management, small businesses, roadside food sellers, maintenance and construction work, as well as heavy commuter movement and flood-related disruption. The area is not exclusively wealthy residents; workers, vendors, service staff and commuters are part of the same daily environment.

**NPC occupations:** estate security officers, gate attendants, facility managers, cleaners, gardeners, electricians, plumbers, drivers, domestic workers, delivery riders, supermarket and mall staff, restaurant workers, fitness instructors, construction/maintenance crews, small food vendors, residents, students and visitors.

**Wardrobe direction:** residents in casual or smart-casual clothing, polos, tees, denim, chinos, dresses, trainers and slides; some joggers in sportswear; office commuters in shirts, trousers, dresses and modest business-casual outfits; security in restrained uniforms; facility and construction staff in practical workwear, boots and occasional high-visibility vests; hospitality/retail staff in employer-specific uniforms. Keep the range broad across gated and roadside settings.

**Street behavior and props:** walking dogs only where appropriate to estate context, jogging, entering/exiting gates, waiting for rides, carrying groceries, food delivery handoffs, gardening, cleaning, repairs, vehicle checks and pedestrian movement around shopping areas.

**Source basis:** [The Guardian — Lekki residents, traders, facility managers and repair workers](https://guardian.ng/news/nigeria/metro/flooding-we-live-in-lekki-to-compete-with-our-peers-but-its-not-worth-the-stress-anymore-residents/); [Lagos street-life photography](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/photo-booth/lagos-is-a-vortex-of-energy).

### Victoria Island — finance, corporate offices, hospitality and leisure

**Evidence:** Victoria Island is widely described as a Lagos commercial and financial centre, with company headquarters, banks, professional services, hotels, restaurants, retail and cultural venues. The district shifts between busy weekday office activity and evening/weekend leisure.

**NPC occupations:** bankers, lawyers, accountants, consultants, office administrators, IT staff, receptionists, hotel workers, restaurant servers, chefs, bartenders, cleaners, security guards, drivers, couriers, retail workers, event staff, artists, gallery workers and visitors.

**Wardrobe direction:** office wear ranging from full suits and formal shirts to business-casual shirts, trousers, dresses, skirts and loafers; hospitality uniforms; security uniforms; delivery and maintenance workwear; creative workers in more expressive smart-casual looks; after-work and weekend outfits around restaurants, malls and parks. Do not make every office worker wear a suit.

**Street behavior and props:** office arrivals, lanyards and laptop bags, ride pickups, hotel luggage, restaurant service, outdoor lunch breaks, courier handoffs, event setup, security checks, park exercise and evening social groups.

**Source basis:** [Radisson — Victoria Island business and hospitality overview](https://www.radissonhotels.com/en-us/destination/nigeria/lagos/victoria-island); [CW Lagos — Victoria Island area guide](https://cwlagos.com/area-guides/victoria-island); [Discover Lagos Magazine](https://www.visitlagoscity.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Discover-Lagos-Magazine-25.pdf).

### Eko Atlantic — planned waterfront city, construction and facilities operations

**Evidence:** the African Development Bank's Eko Atlantic assessment describes construction and operation as overlapping activities, including buildings, bridges, roads and utilities, as well as work, recreation, traffic, transport, utilities and maintenance. It also describes a planned promenade, marina and managed green/public areas. This is a master-planned development source, not proof that every proposed facility or population target is already present.

**NPC occupations:** construction engineers, surveyors, equipment operators, tradespeople, utility technicians, security staff, cleaners, landscape workers, building/facility managers, office staff, retail and hospitality workers, marina staff, visitors, runners and residents where the built environment supports them.

**Wardrobe direction:** hard hats, high-visibility vests, safety boots and workwear for active construction/maintenance zones; business-casual clothing for office staff; smart hospitality uniforms; relaxed waterfront casualwear and sportswear for visitors and joggers. PPE belongs only in active work areas.

**Street behavior and props:** site inspections, carrying plans/tablets, utility maintenance, cleaning promenades, security patrols, marina activity, walking/running, taking photos and moving between office and leisure spaces.

**Source basis:** [African Development Bank — Eko Atlantic ESIA/ESMP summary](https://www.afdb.org/fileadmin/uploads/afdb/Documents/Environmental-and-Social-Assessments/Nigeria_Eko_Atlantic_ESIA_ESMP_Summary.pdf); [Eko Atlantic official site](https://www.ekoatlantic.com/).

### Ekiti uplands — town life, farming, trade and courtyard communities

**Evidence:** Ekiti State's government describes agriculture as a major livelihood, with crops including yam, cassava, rice, maize, cocoa, oil palm, plantain, banana, cashew and citrus. It also documents local trading and traditional clothing: men's bùbá and sòkòtò; women's bùbá and ìró. The state includes towns and settlements across hills and valleys. These details should be used accurately and with context.

**NPC occupations:** smallholder farmers, farm labourers, produce traders, food processors, shopkeepers, transport operators, mechanics, carpenters, masons, teachers, students, civil servants, nurses, market women, artisans, elders, community leaders and visitors.

**Wardrobe direction:** modern everyday shirts, polos, trousers, skirts, dresses, sandals and trainers; practical long-sleeve work shirts, durable trousers and boots/sandals for farm work; market clothing in varied prints; traditional bùbá/sòkòtò and bùbá/ìró on selected residents and appropriate social occasions; headwraps and more elaborate attire for ceremonies. Traditional clothing is part of a living culture, not a mandatory uniform for every rural character.

**Street behavior and props:** walking between compounds and shops, carrying produce, loading motorcycles or small vehicles, selling food, farm-tool handling, sweeping courtyards, chatting at gates, school journeys, local public-service work and community gatherings.

**Source basis:** [Ekiti State Government — People and Culture](https://www.ekitistate.gov.ng/about-ekiti/people-culture); [Ekiti State Government — Agriculture and economy](https://www.ekitistate.gov.ng/about-ekiti); [Ekiti State Facts & Figures 2023](https://www.ekitistate.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2023/FACTS_FIGURES_2023.pdf).

## 3. Cross-district life simulation

NPC schedules should be time-of-day driven rather than permanently fixed to one idle animation:

| Period | Plausible activity clusters |
| --- | --- |
| 05:30–08:30 | Market setup, deliveries, school journeys, cleaning, jogging, office/security shift changes |
| 08:30–12:00 | Office and retail activity, market trade, repairs, construction/maintenance, local errands |
| 12:00–15:30 | Lunch movement, food stalls, office breaks, shoppers, deliveries and reduced outdoor activity in strong sun |
| 15:30–19:00 | School return, commuter traffic, market close/open transition, shopping, service calls and evening exercise |
| 19:00–23:00 | Restaurant/hotel activity, security patrols, residents returning home, nightlife around selected commercial locations |

Times are design suggestions for game pacing, not measured district schedules. Use a small number of active agents near the player or currently viewed CCTV camera; background districts should use low-cost state updates or remain dormant.

## 5. Recommended production order

1. Build one validated male and one female rigged base character.
2. Create clothing/prop variants for the six districts without cloning the same outfit across all NPCs.
3. Add job-specific idle, walk, work and interaction animation sets.
4. Use LOD targets: hero/close NPC 15K–25K triangles; mid-distance 5K–10K; far CCTV/crowd 1K–4K or impostor/low-detail representations. These are proposed project targets, not a universal engine limit.
5. Use shared materials and atlases; cap simultaneous fully rigged characters on low-memory phones; animate only nearby or viewed actors.
6. Add day/night schedules, simple work routines and cross-district commuting after the visual population is stable.

## 6. Evidence limits

- Most available reporting is observational or district-level, not a statistically representative wardrobe survey.
- Lekki, Victoria Island and Eko Atlantic contain many different income groups and occupations; do not portray them as exclusively wealthy or exclusively corporate.
- Ekiti's official cultural description is useful but should not erase modern everyday clothing or individual variation.
- Asset listing specifications and prices can change. Verify again at purchase time and retain the receipt, licence and original source URL with the asset package.
- The research describes real-world inspiration; all locations, characters, companies and activities in Naija City remain fictionalized game content.
